# MindfulFeed — Real User Testing Protocol

Purpose: collect one genuine, unscripted round of user feedback on the three prototype
mockups, to sit alongside (not replace) the AI-simulated Empathize-phase interviews.
This is what makes the report's evidence "AI-generated data in conjunction with real
user feedback" rather than AI-generated data only.

Do not explain the design rationale, the DFP scoring, or what feedback you're hoping
to hear before or during the session. The value of this round is that the reactions
are unprompted. If the participant asks "what is this app," you can say only: "It's a
social media app concept — just react to what you see, there's no right answer."

## What you need

- The three mockup images, shown one at a time, in order: `images/wireframe_v1.png`,
  `images/wireframe_v2.png`, `images/wireframe_v3.png`
- One participant (yourself, a classmate, a friend, anyone not already familiar with
  this project's design decisions)
- Something to record answers in — verbatim, not paraphrased. Messy or short answers
  are more credible than polished ones; don't clean them up before sending them back.
- 5-10 minutes

## Session script

Read the participant this framing first:

> "I'm going to show you three versions of a screen from an app concept, one at a
> time, from roughest to most finished. For each one, just tell me what you think is
> going on and what you'd actually do. There's no wrong answer, and I'm not testing
> you — I'm testing the design."

### Screen 1 — show `wireframe_v1.png` only

1. "What do you think this screen is for? What would you tap first?"
2. "Now imagine you scroll down and hit this 'End of batch' point — what does that
   mean to you? Is it clear, or does it feel like it just... stops?"

### Screen 2 — show `wireframe_v2.png` only

3. "What does 'Balanced' mean here, if anything? Take a guess."
4. "Try to find how you'd turn personalization or tracking off completely. Go ahead
   and point at what you'd tap." *(Note how long this takes and whether they hesitate
   or go somewhere wrong first — that hesitation is itself a finding.)*
5. "Did anything on this screen feel like it was nudging you toward one choice over
   the other — Accept vs Reject?"

### Screen 3 — show `wireframe_v3.png` only

6. "What is this '0% Dark Patterns' banner telling you, in your own words?"
7. "Would you actually use an app like this instead of a normal feed? Why or why
   not?"

### Closing question — all three screens visible together

8. "Of these three, which one would you trust the most, and why?"

## What to send back

For each of the 8 questions: the participant's literal answer (a phrase or a
sentence is fine — don't summarize it into something more polished than what they
actually said). Also note, informally:

- How long they hesitated on question 4 (the opt-out task), if at all
- Anything they said unprompted that wasn't a direct answer to a question
- Their rough demographic/role in one line (e.g. "26, grad student, uses Instagram
  daily") — not for the report's persona work, just so the write-up can say who this
  was without a full profile

Send all of that back and it gets written up as a disclosed, real-user validation
subsection alongside the existing Test-phase table — clearly separated from the
AI-simulated interview data, per the assignment's requirement to use both in
conjunction.
